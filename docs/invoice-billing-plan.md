# Pay by invoice (self-serve) — plan

Status: **planned, not built; IV0 spike run 2026-11-26 (findings in §9, one check still open). I9 and I10 agreed 2026-11-26.** I9 (abuse limits) agreed 2026-11-26; I1–I8 are proposed until the owner confirms them. Written 2026-11-26.
Related: `docs/onboarding-plan.md` §13 (Stripe), `docs/stripe-setup.md`, `docs/subscription-plan.md`.

## 1. What this is

Today an organisation can only pay by card: the plan step opens Stripe Checkout (`mode=subscription`, `payment_method_collection=always`, 14-day trial) and Stripe charges the card at each renewal.
Many companies cannot or will not put a card on a supplier's site: they pay a **bank transfer against an invoice**, often through accounts payable and with a purchase order number.

Self-serve invoicing means an owner can choose **Pay by invoice** on the plan step, give company billing details, and have a subscription that Stripe bills by **emailing an invoice** (hosted page with UK bank-transfer details, card still possible) due in N days. No person at Fortress is involved.

## 2. Why this is more than a switch

- **Stripe Checkout cannot do it.** Checkout in subscription mode always collects a payment method; it has no `send_invoice` option. So the invoice path does **not** use Checkout: Fortress collects the billing details in its own form and the API creates the customer details and the subscription directly (`subscriptions.create` with `collection_method: 'send_invoice'`, `days_until_due`).
- **Nothing proves the customer will pay.** A card at least exists; an invoice is a promise. Access policy, abuse limits and what "overdue" does to access must be decided on purpose (§3).
- **Stripe's overdue behaviour decides how access is cut off** and is the one thing to measure before building (§6, step 0).
- Seats, proration, the Customer Portal, tax, webhooks and the database gate were all written with a card in mind and each needs checking for an invoiced subscription (§5).

## 3. Decisions proposed (owner to confirm)

| # | Decision | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | Trial | **No trial for invoiced subscriptions.** Access starts when the subscription is created; the first invoice is issued at once, due in 14 days. | A trial with no card is 14 free days with nothing at stake. The card route keeps its trial. |
| I2 | Payment terms | **14 days** (`days_until_due`), fixed in code, not chosen by the customer. | Common default. Longer terms can be set by us later per organisation. |
| I3 | Intervals | **Annual and monthly** both allowed. | Monthly invoices are admin-heavy for the customer but it is their choice; annual is nudged in the copy. |
| I4 | When it is overdue | Access follows the **existing past-due grace** (`grace_period_days`, default 7) counted from the due date, then the organisation is locked as for a failed card. | One rule for both payment routes, already built and tested. |
| I5 | Who may choose it | Any **owner**, but only after entering **legal company name, billing address and a billing email**; VAT number optional. | I6 | Changing route | An organisation may move **from card to invoice and back** only at a renewal, from the Subscription page, by cancelling at period end and starting the other kind (no live switching of a running subscription's collection method in the first version). | Avoids half-states; Stripe supports switching but the edge cases (open invoices, proration) are not worth it yet. |
| I7 | Purchase order | Optional **PO reference** on the form, put on every invoice (Stripe invoice custom field). | The most common thing accounts payable ask for. |
| I8 | Seat changes (**replaced by I10**) | Extra seats are **added to the next invoice** (prorated), not invoiced instantly; removing seats credits the next invoice. | Matches how invoiced customers expect to be billed; avoids a surprise mid-month invoice. | I9 | Abuse limits (agreed 2026-11-26, see §3a) | Seat cap until the first invoice is paid; data-leaving features closed while it is unpaid; 3 days' grace for invoiced customers; one invoiced organisation per company; operator alerts. | Keeps the self-serve start while capping the worst case at the seats on one invoice. |

## 3a. Abuse limits (I9)

The worst case without limits is up to 21 days of use (14 to pay + 7 grace) with nothing paid, repeated with new organisations. The agreed limits:

1. **Seat cap until the first invoice is paid.** An invoiced organisation can hold only the seats on its first invoice. While **any** invoice is open or overdue, `app.check_seat_change` refuses a seat increase (`QM009`, "pay the open invoice first"); decreases are always allowed. After the first invoice is paid the cap is lifted.
2. **Data-leaving features closed while the first invoice is unpaid:** creating **reporting keys** (Power BI), **activating or running integrations that write out**, and **bulk CSV exports** answer "available once your first invoice is paid". Reading in the app, importing data in, and everything else stay open. Worked out by one database function, `app.invoice_first_payment_pending(org)` (true for an invoiced organisation whose first invoice is not yet paid), so every screen and route asks the same thing.
3. **Shorter grace for invoiced customers: 3 days** after the due date (card customers keep 7). Stored as the organisation's `grace_period_days` when the invoice subscription is applied; the validity rule in §4.3 uses it.
4. **One invoiced organisation per company.** The legal name (ignoring case and punctuation) and the VAT number are kept on the subscription; a second organisation choosing invoice with the same VAT number, or the same legal name, is refused with "an organisation for this company already exists: ask its owner to invite you". The VAT number must verify with Stripe (Stripe checks UK and EU numbers when a tax id is added) before the subscription is created. An operator can lift the block for a genuine second entity.
5. **Operator alerts.** A notice to platform operators when an organisation starts an invoiced subscription, and when its **first** invoice passes its due date; both also appear on the `/platform` organisations tab (route, first invoice paid or not, overdue days). Never emailed to the customer.

**Not built (kept for if real abuse appears):** refusing free-mail billing addresses, pay-first access (nothing until the first invoice is paid), operator approval of the first invoice, a card on file as a fallback, archiving unpaid invoiced organisations sooner than the existing job does.

## 3b. Seat increases are billed at once (I10)

**Why.** The IV0 spike (§9) showed that a seat increase with `create_prorations` creates only **pending invoice items**; nothing is invoiced until the next invoice, which is the renewal. The card route uses the same setting today. So an organisation can add seats and use them until the renewal: up to **a year** on an annual plan, a month on a monthly one. Dropping the seats again before renewal does not avoid the charge (the pending items land on the same invoice), but if that invoice is never paid the seats were free until access stops.

**Rule (both routes).**
1. **An increase is invoiced immediately** (`proration_behavior: always_invoice`): for an invoiced subscription a new invoice for the prorated amount, due in the usual 14 days (and finalised by us at once, as in §9); for a card subscription the card is charged now.
2. **A decrease stays a credit** on the next invoice (`create_prorations`); nothing is refunded, so there is nothing to game. Seats in use cannot be reduced below the people holding them (existing `check_seat_change`).
3. **§3a still applies on top:** no further increase while any invoice is open or overdue (so an unpaid increase invoice blocks the next increase), and an overdue one follows the same grace and lock as any other invoice.
4. **The preview says what will happen:** "Invoiced now: £X (due <date>)" for an invoiced subscription, "Charged now: £X" for a card, and "Credit of £X on your next invoice" for a decrease. `previewSeats` previews with the same proration setting the change will use, and its `amountDue` is the amount collected now.
5. **Card customers: seats only if the payment succeeds** (`payment_behavior: pending_if_incomplete`), so a declined card leaves the subscription unchanged. **Invoiced subscriptions cannot do this:** Stripe refuses `pending_if_incomplete` when the collection method is `send_invoice` (checked in the sandbox, §9). The invoiced route therefore grants the seats at once and relies on the increase invoice (due in 14 days), the §3a rule (no further increase while any invoice is open) and the 3-day grace.

**Cost.** Card customers see a charge when they add seats instead of at the next renewal: a behaviour change to say in the release notes and the seat dialog. It can ship **on its own, before the invoice work** (slice IV-S below), because it changes only `StripeGateway.updateSeats` / `previewSeatChange` and the seat dialog.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 The customer's path
Plan step (wizard) and Settings → Subscription → Start subscription gain a first choice: **Pay by card** (today, with the 14-day trial) or **Pay by invoice**.
Invoice path: choose seats and interval as now → **Billing details** form (company name, address, billing email, optional VAT number and PO reference, a plain-words summary "We will email an invoice for £X to Y, payable within 14 days by bank transfer or card; your seats are available now") → **Start subscription** → the same confirmation/summary as a card customer, plus the first invoice (amount, due date, **Pay this invoice** link to Stripe's hosted page, bank details).

### 4.2 API (`api/src/billing/`)
- `POST /billing/invoice-subscription` (owner only, `subscription.manage`; strict body: `seats`, `interval`, `company {name, address…, email, vatNumber?}`, `poReference?`, `clientKey`).
  1. Same guards as `checkout()`: owner, no live subscription, `assertNoSeatProblems`, prices from `app.stripe_price_for`, `invoice_billing_allowed`.
  2. Update the Stripe customer (name, email, address) and add the tax id (`customers.createTaxId`; an invalid number is a form error, not a silent drop).
  3. `subscriptions.create({ collection_method: 'send_invoice', days_until_due: 14, automatic_tax, payment_settings: { payment_method_types: ['customer_balance','card'], payment_method_options: { customer_balance: { funding_type: 'bank_transfer', bank_transfer: { type: 'gb_bank_transfer' } } } }, metadata, … }, { idempotencyKey })`, **then finalise the first invoice ourselves** (`invoices.finalize`; Stripe leaves it a draft for about an hour otherwise, §9), PO reference as an invoice custom field.
  4. Re-read the subscription from Stripe and apply it through the existing `syncSubscription` (Stripe stays the source of truth; a retry with the same `clientKey` returns the same subscription).
- `StripeGateway` gains `createInvoiceSubscription(...)` and `updateCustomerBilling(...)`; `SubscriptionSnapshot` gains `collectionMethod` and `daysUntilDue`; `toSubscriptionSnapshot` maps them. The fake gateway in tests gets the same methods.
- `SubscriptionOverview` gains `paymentRoute: 'card' | 'invoice'`, `billingContact`, `purchaseOrder`, and `openInvoice {id, number, amountDue, dueOn, hostedUrl}` (from the latest open invoice) so the page can show what is owed.
- The webhook list already covers `invoice.*`; add `invoice.overdue` is **not** an event Stripe sends reliably, so overdue is worked out in the database (§4.3), not from an event.

### 4.3 Database (one migration)
- `organisation_subscriptions`: `collection_method text` (`card` default | `invoice`), `payment_terms_days`, `billing_contact_email`, `purchase_order`. `organisations`/settings: `invoice_billing_allowed boolean default true` (platform-owned; set by the operator).
- `invoice_first_payment_pending(org)`, the one-per-company check (`billing_company_key` = normalised legal name, `vat_number`, unique among live invoiced subscriptions unless an operator lifts it with `app.set_invoice_company_exempt`), the seat rule in `app.check_seat_change` and the operator notices of §3a (event `platform_invoice_alerts`, written by `app.stripe_apply_subscription` and the daily reconcile).
- `app.stripe_apply_subscription` takes the new snapshot fields (patched from its live definition with `pg_temp.patch_once`, marker-guarded, as the other migrations do).
- **Validity for an invoiced subscription** (`app.organisation_subscription_valid`): valid while `active`, and **not valid when the oldest open invoice's due date + grace is in the past** — even if Stripe still says `active`. This makes access independent of exactly how Stripe labels an overdue send-invoice subscription (§6 step 0 tells us whether it is also needed for `past_due`). Needs `billing_invoices.due_on` (check it is stored; add if not) and an index for the lookup.
- **Controller invariant and `QM009` seat rules are unchanged** (the first Controller seat goes to the owner who started it: record the starter as `stripe_start_checkout` does, with a synthetic checkout id, or add the user id parameter).
- Operator functions: `app.set_invoice_billing_allowed(org, allowed, reason)` (platform admin, audited); the operator panel's organisations tab shows the route and any overdue invoice.
- Tests: SQL test for validity (open invoice just before due → valid; past due + grace → locked; paid → valid again; card route unaffected), idempotent apply, a card customer never reads as invoiced.

### 4.4 Seats and the rest of billing
- `previewSeats` / `changeSeats` already go through Stripe; **increases are invoiced at once and decreases credited (I10, §3b)**, so the preview copy says "invoiced now" / "charged now" / "credit on your next invoice".
- **Customer Portal:** for invoiced customers it shows invoices and billing details; "update payment method" is meaningless. Keep the portal configuration as is and hide the "payment method" wording on our own page for the invoice route.
- **Stripe Tax:** unchanged (`automatic_tax`); a tax id entered on the form is what lets a VAT-registered business be invoiced correctly.
- **Reminders and dunning:** use Stripe's own invoice emails (send on finalise, reminder before due, overdue reminder) — turn on in the Stripe dashboard (Billing → Subscriptions and emails). Fortress adds an in-app banner (`SubscriptionBanner`): "Invoice INV-… for £X is due on …" and, once overdue, "access stops on …" (reuses `subscriptionWarning`).
- **Failure to pay:** past grace the organisation is locked exactly as a failed card is today (`SubscriptionNeeded` / `lapsed`); paying the invoice (Stripe marks it paid → webhook → re-read) unlocks it at once.

### 4.5 Frontend
`PlanStep` and Settings → Subscription: payment route choice (`SlidingToggle`, two options with a one-line explanation each); `BillingDetailsForm` (custom pickers, country combo, validation, required marks); Subscription page shows the route, the open invoice with **Pay this invoice** and **Download**, and "Next invoice" for card customers as today; `lib/billing.js` remembers the choice across a cancelled step as it does for seats. Wizard Review says "Invoice, payable within 14 days" for the plan card. Optimistic UI does not apply (creating a subscription is a step Stripe decides); the button uses `loading`.

## 5. What must be re-checked, not assumed

3. Invoice settings: PO custom field, bank-transfer details for `gb_bank_transfer` on the account (needs the payment method enabled on the Stripe account; GB bank transfer is available to UK accounts), currency GBP only.
4. Proration on a seat increase for `send_invoice` (pending invoice items, not an immediate invoice).
5. Cancellation at period end and the final invoice.
6. Everything in `docs/stripe-setup.md` §5, repeated for the invoice route in the sandbox, then the live account.

## 7. Out of scope (first version)
Custom payment terms per customer in the UI, credit notes and refunds screens, purchase-order approval workflows, statements, payment by direct debit (BACS Direct Debit is a separate Stripe payment method with its own mandate step and is a better long-term answer for monthly customers), multiple currencies, switching a running subscription between card and invoice, our own overdue emails (Stripe sends them).

## 8. Risks
- **Free use before paying** (I1/I4): now up to 14 + 3 days, on the seats of one invoice, with no way to take data out, one organisation per company, and operators told (I9, §3a). If abuse still appears: free-mail refusal, pay-first access, a minimum first-invoice amount.
- **Stripe dashboard settings are outside the code** and silently change behaviour (unpaid-invoice handling, reminder emails). They are written into `stripe-setup.md` and checked in IVX.
- **Tax:** a wrong or missing address/VAT number produces wrong VAT on an invoice that cannot be edited once finalised; the form validates address completeness and the tax id with Stripe at entry.
- **Bank transfer reconciliation** is Stripe's (customer balance); a payment that arrives without the invoice reference can sit unapplied. Stripe handles most cases automatically; the rest is a manual Stripe-dashboard task for the owner and is not built here.

## 9. IV0 findings (sandbox, 2026-11-26, test clock `clock_1UgBqWK5lGCrs5Si5RKqu1PI`, customer `cus_Vhb2CmSlHGBeJc`, subscription `sub_1UgBquK5lGCrs5SiZAbTozoM`)

Made with the Stripe connector against the **sandbox** only (never the live account): a customer attached to a test clock, a Contributor monthly subscription × 2 with `collection_method: send_invoice`, `days_until_due: 14`, `automatic_tax` on and bank transfer (`customer_balance`, `gb_bank_transfer`) plus card as the payment methods.

**Observed**
1. **The subscription is `active` the moment it is created**, with no payment (no trial, no incomplete state). Access can start at once, as I1 assumes.
2. **The first invoice is created as a `draft` and Stripe finalises it about an hour later** (`automatically_finalizes_at` = created + 1 h). It does **not** finalise immediately. We therefore finalise it ourselves right after creating the subscription (`POST /v1/invoices/{id}/finalize`), after which it is `open` with a number, a **hosted payment page** (`hosted_invoice_url`) and a PDF (`invoice_pdf`). Its `due_date` is 14 days from creation either way.
3. **`gb_bank_transfer` and card are accepted** on the sandbox account (the subscription and invoice carry `payment_method_types: [customer_balance, card]`), so the hosted page can offer UK bank details. The live account has not been checked: **turn on Bank transfers in the live Stripe dashboard (Settings → Payment methods) before launch.**
4. **Tax:** automatic tax is complete with 0 % and `taxability_reason: not_collecting` (no registration yet, as in `stripe-setup.md`). A VAT number added later changes nothing until a registration exists.
5. **A seat increase does not create an invoice.** Raising 2 → 3 seats with `create_prorations` leaves **pending invoice items on the customer** (−£20 "unused time on 2 ×" and +£30 "remaining time on 3 ×"), to be added to the **next** invoice; `latest_invoice` stays the first one. This confirms I8, and it also shows the unused-time credit **refers to the first invoice even though that is still unpaid**, so the net extra (£10) is only collected at the next renewal. With §3a's rule (no seat increase while any invoice is open) this cannot be reached before the first invoice is paid.

**From Stripe's documentation (not observed: the connector cannot advance a test clock)**
6. A `send_invoice` subscription becomes **`past_due` when its invoice is unpaid at the due date**; the Dashboard setting (Billing → Subscriptions and emails → Manage failed payments) can then move it to `canceled` or `unpaid`, or leave it `past_due`. Status resolution can use the most recent invoice or all invoices. Paying the governing invoice makes the subscription `active` again, **even after the due date**.
7. The invoice itself reads `past_due`, and an operator can mark it `uncollectible` or `void`; voiding the first invoice of a `past_due` subscription makes it `active`.

**What this changes in the design**
- **§4.3's extra validity rule (open invoice + grace) is probably unnecessary.** Stripe itself makes the subscription `past_due` at the due date, and the existing machinery already treats `past_due` with a grace measured from when we learn of it (`past_due_since`, `grace_period_days`), so setting the 3-day grace (I9.3) is enough. **Keep the extra rule only if the check below shows the status does not move.**
- **Set Stripe's "Manage failed payments" to leave the subscription `past_due`** (do not auto-cancel), so Fortress, not Stripe, decides when access stops, and a late payment simply restores it. Write this into `stripe-setup.md`.
- The API creates the subscription, **finalises the first invoice**, then reads both back and applies them through `syncSubscription` as before.

**Observed after advancing the test clock to 12 Dec 2026 (2026-11-27, sandbox)**
8. **The subscription went `past_due`** at the due date (event `customer.subscription.updated`, status `past_due`), with the invoices still `open`, `attempted: false`, nothing paid or voided. The clock events were `test_clock.advancing` then `test_clock.ready`; Stripe also re-sent the open invoices (`invoice.sent`, `invoice.finalized`, `invoice.updated`). So **§4.3's extra validity rule is not needed to notice an overdue invoice**: the existing `past_due` machinery with the 3-day grace (I9.3) is enough, and the rule stays only as a backstop. "Manage failed payments" in the sandbox is evidently set to leave the subscription `past_due` (it did not cancel or go `unpaid`).
9. `latest_invoice` was a seat-change invoice from the IV-S experiments (`subscription_update`, due the same day), not the first invoice, so a status that follows "the most recent invoice" is governed by that one: pay or void **all** open invoices to clear `past_due` (the plan's rule that no increase is allowed while any invoice is open keeps this to one).

**Not yet observed:** a further advance (about 20 Dec) to see whether anything moves it past `past_due`, reminder emails, an unmatched bank transfer, cancel at period end on an unpaid invoice, and a real bank transfer paying an invoice (the connector cannot fund a test bank transfer). Bank transfers in the **live** account were switched on by the owner on 2026-11-27 and have not been checked from here (the live account is never touched in this work). The clock deletes itself on 26 Dec 2026.

### 9a. IV-S checks (sandbox, 2026-11-27)
- **Invoiced, `always_invoice`:** accepted. It creates a **new invoice** (`billing_reason: subscription_update`) as a **draft** that Stripe would finalise in about an hour (we finalise it ourselves), due 14 days after creation. **It also sweeps in any proration items already pending**, so the invoice nets to the true difference (2 → 4 seats = £20 for the month: −£30 + £40 − £20 + £30).
- **Invoiced, `payment_behavior: pending_if_incomplete`:** **refused** ("cannot set payment_behavior to pending_if_incomplete when the collection method is send_invoice").
- **Card, `always_invoice` + `pending_if_incomplete`, good card:** accepted; the update applied at once (quantity 3, `status: active`, `pending_update: null`) and the invoice for the extra seat (credit £20, charge £30, net £10) was **finalised and paid in the same call**.
- **Card, declined payment:** **not observed.** The connector cannot attach a second payment method to a customer or create a setup intent, so a card that attaches and then declines could not be put on a subscription that already existed. To observe it: in the sandbox dashboard open customer `cus_VhlFAR4RfujUMq` ("IV0 Card Good Ltd"), add the test card 4000 0000 0000 0341 (attaches, then declines) as the default payment method, and change its subscription's seats; or watch it in the IV-S end-to-end run. Stripe documents that with `pending_if_incomplete` an update whose payment fails stays a `pending_update` and the subscription keeps its quantity.

## 10. IV1 as built (2026-11-27)

Migration `20260463100000_invoice_billing.sql`, test `supabase/tests/invoice_billing.test.sql` (five deliberate breaks tried and caught: the seat rule, the overdue access rule, the reporting-key rule, the company check, the table backstop). **Not applied live yet** (history row `('20260463100000', 'invoice_billing')` after it is). Pushing the API before it is safe only because IV2 has not been written: nothing selects the new columns yet.

What it does, and where it differs from §4.3:
- **Columns:** `organisation_subscriptions` gains `collection_method` (`card` default | `invoice`), `payment_terms_days`, `billing_contact_email`, `purchase_order`, `billing_legal_name`, `billing_company_key`, `billing_vat_number`; `organisations` gains the operator-owned `invoice_billing_allowed`, `invoice_company_exempt`, `invoice_billing_note`. The invoice start is recorded in `stripe_checkout_sessions` (id `inv_<client key>`, the billing details in `billing_details`, the owner who started it for the first Controller seat), so `app.stripe_apply_subscription` reads the details from that row when it is given the id (a webhook that arrives first sets only the route; the API's own apply then fills the details).
- **`app.stripe_start_invoice(org, user, client key, interval, seats, details)`** (API login only): owner only, refused with a live subscription, route allowed, company details and address required, one invoiced organisation per company, a retry with the same key is the same record (`created: false`).
- **One invoiced organisation per company:** `app.company_key` (name without case, punctuation, spaces or a trailing Ltd / Limited) and `app.vat_key`; `app.invoice_company_problems` (internal) and `app.check_invoice_company` (for the form, `subscription.manage`); a trigger on the table is the backstop for two applying at once; an operator lifts it with `app.set_invoice_company_exempt(org, exempt, reason)`. VAT numbers are **not** verified with Stripe here (that is IV2: the API adds the tax id and reports a refusal).
- **Seat cap (§3a.1):** `app.check_seat_change` refuses an **increase** on an invoiced subscription while any invoice is open or draft (the message names it) or the first invoice is unpaid; decreases and unchanged seats are always fine.
- **Data-leaving features (§3a.2):** `app.invoice_first_payment_pending(org)` (members only) and `_internal` (no check). Creating a **reporting key** refuses with **`QM040`** ("available once your first invoice is paid"); IV2 maps `QM040` to `403 payable_first`. There are no write-out integrations in the product (imports only), so nothing else is closed in the database; **bulk CSV exports are client-side and are IV2's to close** (the screen asks the question and hides the button).
- **Grace (§3a.3):** set when the invoice route is first applied: `organisation_settings.grace_period_days` = 3; applying a card snapshot to an invoiced organisation puts it back to 7. An operator can change it afterwards; applying does not touch it otherwise.
- **Access (§4.3):** `app.organisation_subscription_status_ok` now also stops an **invoiced** subscription whose oldest open invoice is past its due date plus the grace days, whatever Stripe still calls the subscription (so the plan's "probably unnecessary" rule was built; the IV0 test-clock check is still worth doing to see whether Stripe's `past_due` agrees). Card subscriptions never reach this rule.
- **Operator alerts (§3a.5):** `platform_invoice_alerts` (one `started` and one `first_invoice_overdue` per organisation; platform-owned like `stripe_events`), `started` written by the apply, `first_invoice_overdue` by `app.system_invoice_overdue_alerts()` (API login only; IV2 calls it from the daily reconcile), `app.platform_invoice_accounts(limit)` for the operator panel (route, first invoice paid, open invoices and amount, days overdue, flags). It is a separate function, not extra columns on `platform_organisations`, so the existing operator endpoint is unchanged. A notice beyond the list (email to operators) is not built.
- **Operator controls:** `app.set_invoice_billing_allowed(org, allowed, reason)`, `app.set_invoice_company_exempt(org, exempt, reason)` (platform admin with MFA, reason required, kept in `invoice_billing_note`, audited through `organisations`).
- **Not in IV1:** the Stripe calls, VAT verification, `POST /billing/invoice-subscription`, overview fields, the daily alert job, the screens (IV2, IV3).
