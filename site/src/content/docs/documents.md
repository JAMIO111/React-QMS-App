Fortress keeps each document as one identity (for example SOP-001) with revisions under it (rev A, rev B and so on). Only one revision is in force at a time. Documents are information: they never block work.

## Set up the types

Under **Settings → Data Management → Documents**, each document type has a name, a prefix, a review interval in months and a suggested content kind. A type numbers its own documents (SOP-001, WI-001). A prefix is fixed once a document uses it. A type you switch off takes no new documents. The built-in Calibration procedure type cannot be switched off.

## Make a document

1. Open **Documentation → Documents** and choose **New document**.
2. Pick the **Type** and the **Content**: written in Fortress, or a file. This cannot change later.
3. Give it a title and an owner (you, unless you choose someone else).
4. If you are bringing an existing document across, switch on **Bringing an existing document across** and type its number and its current revision letter. Numbering carries on from there.

Open the first draft. For a written document, write in the editor (headings, lists, tables, links). It saves as you type. For a file, upload the **Main file**. A PDF is best for reading. Add the editable source as an **Attachment**.

## Review, approve, take effect

1. On the draft, name at least one **reviewer** and the **approver**. The author cannot be either. A reviewer may also approve. The approver list greys out people who cannot approve documents.
2. From the second revision on, fill in the **Change summary**: what changed and why.
3. Choose **Send for review**. The draft gets its letter now. A **Still needed** list tells you what is missing.
4. Each reviewer chooses **Accept** or **Send back** with a comment. A sent-back draft returns to the author, and a new round starts when it is sent again.
5. When every reviewer has accepted, the approver chooses **Approve** and sets **Takes effect on**, today or later. A document that was approved for a future date takes effect on that day. The revision before it is then superseded.

To change a document in force, choose **Start a new revision**. It begins as a copy of the revision in force.

## Readers

On the document, add **Readers**: departments, roles or named people. The list of who was asked is fixed on the day a revision takes effect, and each new revision asks them again. You can set **Days readers have to acknowledge it**, add a reader later, and switch on **Restricted** so only readers and document managers can open it.

Readers see what is waiting in **Documentation → My documents** and choose **Acknowledge**, with an optional note. The document page shows how many have acknowledged and how many are overdue.

## Keep it current

- **Periodic review.** Each document has a next review date from its type's interval, or its own. Record **No change needed** with a note, or start a revision.
- **Change requests.** Anyone who can read a document can ask for a change. Managers use **Accept into a revision** or **Decline**, and the person who asked is told. See them under **Documentation → Change requests**.
- **Where it applies.** Link a document to parts, part families, machines or operations. It then shows on those pages and on jobs, for information.

## External documents

Switch on **External** when you create it for a customer specification, a standard or a supplier manual. It is always a file and is not reviewed here. Use **Record this issue** with their revision and the date it applies from.

## Copies and withdrawing

A PDF opens as a controlled copy, stamped on every page with who opened it, when, and whether it is in force. A written document prints with the same stamp. Anything saved or printed is uncontrolled after that day.

**Withdraw** takes a document out of force everywhere, with a reason. A revision started later can bring it back into use.

## Good to know

- Approving, recording an external issue and withdrawing need the right to issue documents. Writing drafts needs the right to manage them.
- A revision in force is never edited. Start a new one.
- A draft that was never issued can be discarded with a reason. Its number is not reused.
- Reading and acknowledging a document can show as evidence on a person's [competence](/help/competence) record. It never assesses them.
